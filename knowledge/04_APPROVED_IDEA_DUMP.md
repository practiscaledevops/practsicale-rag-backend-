# 16 TRANSCRIPT TO OUTPUT METHOD

## Objective

When the user supplies a raw video transcript, do not simply clean up the wording.

Mine the transcript for ideas.

## Step 1: Extract factual moments

Identify:
- decisions
- mistakes
- numbers
- outcomes
- conflicts
- changes
- realizations
- rules the speaker follows

Do not invent connective details.

## Step 2: Find the deeper lesson

For each factual moment, ask:

"What does this prove?"

Example:

Raw idea:
We kept hiring people but the company became harder to manage.

Surface output:
"Hiring more people can create management problems."

Better insight:
"Hiring gives you people. Systems turn them into a company."

## Step 3: Find contrast

Useful contrast pairs:

- result / cause
- people / systems
- growth / chaos
- explaining / enforcing
- money / skill
- position / sacrifice
- attention / trust
- hiring / leadership
- activity / outcome
- temporary fix / permanent system

Do not force a contrast when the source does not support one.

## Step 4: Compress

Draft the insight.

Then remove:
- setup the audience already understands
- filler
- adjectives
- repeated explanation
- unnecessary qualifiers

Preserve meaning and truth.

## Step 5: Diversify

If asked for multiple outputs, each should mine a different lesson or angle.

Bad:
10 versions of "systems are important."

Better:
One about systems.
One about hiring.
One about leadership.
One about recurring problems.
One about growth exposing weakness.
One about documentation.
One about delegation.
One about accountability.
One about CEO dependency.
One about scale.

Only use angles supported by the source.

## Step 6: Protect factual integrity

Never invent:
- timelines
- backstories
- motivations
- employee history
- conversations
- emotions
- results

If the transcript gives only the result, use the result.

Do not manufacture a cinematic story around it.
