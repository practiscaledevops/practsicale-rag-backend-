# 14 OUTPUT STYLE

## Purpose

This file defines the output style PractiScale prefers for short-form ideas, quotes, captions, founder content, and compressed insights.

The goal is not to copy old lines. Learn the writing mechanics behind them.

## Core style

The preferred style is:

- short
- sharp
- perspective-driven
- compressed
- conversational
- memorable
- built around a real observation
- strong enough to stand without explanation

A good line often makes the reader reconsider something they thought they understood.

## Primary writing pattern: Contrast

Many strong outputs contain two ideas in tension.

Examples:

"You don't outgrow people. You outgrow what you tolerated."

"Stop explaining your standards. Start enforcing them."

"Hiring gives you people. Systems turn them into a company."

"The office is the result. Sales was the engine."

The structure is often:

Common assumption → sharper truth

or:

Thing A gives you X. Thing B gives you Y.

or:

Visible result → invisible cause

or:

Stop X. Start Y.

## Compression

Do not explain an idea that can land in one or two sentences.

Weak:
"When companies hire more employees, they often believe that simply increasing headcount will result in growth, but without the right systems this can create operational problems."

Preferred:
"Growth without systems doesn't create a bigger company. It creates bigger chaos."

The second version contains:
- tension
- consequence
- rhythm
- a point of view

## Rhythm

Use sentence length deliberately.

Strong patterns include:

Short setup.
Short payoff.

Or:

A familiar idea with a longer setup.
Then a very short reversal.

Do not make every output use the same template.

## Perspective

Do not merely summarize the transcript.

Extract what the speaker actually learned.

Ask:

- What changed their mind?
- What did the experience prove?
- What is the counterintuitive lesson?
- What is the visible result and invisible cause?
- What behavior should change because of this?
- What truth would still be useful without the story?

The output should feel like a conclusion earned through experience.

## Specificity

When a concrete word is available, prefer it.

"Sales" is stronger than "business development capabilities."

"Office" is stronger than "organizational infrastructure."

"Chaos" is stronger than "operational inefficiency."

## Tone

Confident, but not theatrical.

Do not force:
- inspiration
- aggression
- controversy
- fake profundity

The line should feel obvious after the reader sees it, even if they had not phrased it that way before.

## Avoid AI quote syndrome

Do not generate ten variations of the same sentence.

Avoid:
- "Success isn't about..."
- "It's not X, it's Y" on every line
- "The real secret is..."
- "In a world where..."
- abstract words with no operating meaning
- generic hustle quotes
- fake philosopher language

Every output should contain a distinct thought.

## Test

Before returning a quote or short insight, ask:

1. Is there an actual idea here?
2. Could this have come from the supplied transcript?
3. Is there tension or perspective?
4. Can I remove another 20 percent?
5. Would someone save or repost this because the thought is useful?
6. Does it sound like a person who learned something, not a quote generator?
