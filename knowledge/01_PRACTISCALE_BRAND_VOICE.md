# Sales and Persuasion Philosophy

## 1. Sell the mechanism, not magic

The prospect should understand what happens.

Bad:
"We'll transform your healthcare business."

Better:
"We identify the referral categories that matter in your territory, find relevant organizations, qualify the opportunity, and help you start the relationship."

## 2. Fit beats pressure

A sale is not automatically a win.

If the prospect is clearly not ready, financially uncomfortable, or expects guarantees PractiScale cannot provide, do not write pressure tactics to force the close.

## 3. Specificity reduces skepticism

The more skeptical the audience, the more useful concrete explanation becomes.

Use:
- exact deliverables
- timelines when approved
- actual process
- relevant proof
- boundaries

## 4. Contrast is powerful

Examples:

Lead list vs relationship.

Random broker legs vs relationships with facilities.

Attention vs trust.

Renting demand vs building an asset you can continue nurturing.

Course-only promise vs training connected to real work opportunities.

## 5. Value before the sale

Useful content can demonstrate how PractiScale thinks before asking for money.

Teach enough that the prospect can see competence.

Do not intentionally make educational content vague just to force a call.

## 6. The next step should match awareness

Cold prospect:
earn attention and relevance.

Booked prospect:
increase commitment and reduce uncertainty.

Research buyer:
show insight and make the next problem visible.

High-ticket prospect:
connect the diagnosed problem to implementation.

Existing client:
clarity, confidence, progress, accountability.

## 7. Objections are information

Do not treat objections as enemies.

An objection often reveals:
- missing trust
- missing understanding
- bad timing
- poor fit
- weak proof
- unclear mechanism
- financial constraint

Write to the cause.

## 8. Do not promise what a third party controls

PractiScale can control research, process, qualification, outreach, introductions, follow-up systems, and agreed deliverables.

PractiScale cannot honestly guarantee that a hospital, facility, payer, referral source, patient, or other independent party will behave a certain way unless a specific approved contractual guarantee says otherwise.
