# PractiScale Brand Voice

## Core voice

Direct.
Conversational.
Specific.
Credibility-first.
Human.
Confident without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

The writing should feel spoken.

A reader should be able to imagine a founder, salesperson, or operator saying it naturally.

## Preferred qualities

### 1. Concrete

Weak:
"We help healthcare businesses unlock sustainable growth."

Better:
"We help healthcare owners build relationships with the people already in a position to refer the right patients."

### 2. Proof before hype

Weak:
"Our revolutionary system changes everything."

Better:
Explain the mechanism, show the result, then make the point.

### 3. Controlled emotion

Emotion is welcome when earned.

Do not manufacture drama.

### 4. Short opening

Get to the point quickly.

Avoid three paragraphs of scene-setting before the actual idea.

### 5. Simple vocabulary

Do not use complicated business language when ordinary English is stronger.

### 6. Spoken rhythm

Mix short sentences with longer explanatory ones.

Do not make every sentence the same length.

## Avoid

- corporate jargon
- "cutting-edge"
- "robust"
- "seamless"
- "revolutionary"
- "game-changing"
- "unlock"
- "explode"
- "effortless"
- generic motivational language
- fake scarcity
- fake urgency
- unearned superlatives
- excessive emojis
- excessive headings
- excessive quotation marks
- em dashes
- en dashes
- copy that sounds like a funnel template
- copy that could belong to any agency

## Useful test

Remove the company name.

If the exact copy could be pasted onto 100 agency websites without changing anything, it is too generic.

## Signature lines / beliefs

Use sparingly, not mechanically:

"Being known gets attention. Being trusted gets the call."

"Don't rent everything. Build something you own too."

The purpose is to express a belief, not create a slogan factory.
